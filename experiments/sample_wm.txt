Vaccines help protect people from diseases by introducing a small part of the virus or bacteria into their body without making them sick. This "part" is called an antigen.

When the body meets this antigen, it recognizes it as foreign and mounts an immune response to fight it off. The immune system produces antibodies that recognize the antigen and remember it for future encounters. If the same virus or bacteria enters the body again, these antibodies can quickly identify and destroy it before it causes any illness.

By building up immunity through vaccination, individuals are better prepared to handle potential infections later on if they encounter those specific viruses or bacteria. This means fewer outbreaks and less severe symptoms when someone does get sick with a disease.
